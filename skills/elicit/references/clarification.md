# Clarify a question

Help the user understand the pending decision when they ask for an explanation or show confusion. Choose the technique that addresses the misunderstanding, then ask one rephrased question and wait. Keep the same scope, alternatives, and consequences. Rephrasing does not settle the branch.

## Restore context and purpose

Say what is already settled, what remains open, and why the answer matters. Avoid repeating the whole interview.

**Original:** “What is the retention policy?”

**User:** “Why are you asking that?”

**Rephrase:** “We agreed to keep the scanned letters. We still need to decide what happens to the paper originals, because scanning alone does not authorise disposal. Should the originals be kept after the scans are checked?”

## Replace jargon with observable behaviour

Use the user's established vocabulary and describe what they would see or do. Define an unavoidable technical term once.

**Original:** “Must revocation invalidate cached copies?”

**User:** “What does that mean?”

**Rephrase:** “Someone downloads a letter, then you remove their access. Should they still be able to read the copy already on their device?”

## Contrast concrete outcomes

Show how the viable alternatives differ in the same scenario. Give each a fair description. Explain a recommendation separately. Use an analogy only if it preserves the consequential distinction.

**Original:** “Should a timeout retry the operation?”

**User:** “I can't picture the difference.”

**Rephrase:** “You tap ‘Send’, but the app never receives a reply. Sending again may deliver a duplicate. Waiting may leave the message unsent. Which result should the app favour?”

## Isolate the prerequisite

If the question assumes something the user has not decided or understood, ask that prerequisite first. Return to the original branch afterward.

**Original:** “How should public excerpts be anonymised?”

**User:** “I don't know where to start.”

**Rephrase:** “First we need to settle whether excerpts may be public at all. May an excerpt be published without the people mentioned agreeing?”

## Make the conflict tangible

State the incompatible claims and walk through a counterexample. Challenge the premise without treating the user's preference as a mistake.

**User:** “Removing names guarantees nobody can identify the people.”

**Response:** “A letter could still identify someone through their address, job, or family relationships. Removing names alone cannot guarantee anonymity. Would you require agreement before publishing an excerpt?”

If your interpretation caused the confusion, correct it first: “I read ‘share’ as public posting, but you meant sending privately. Who may receive a private copy?”

When talking cannot settle the choice, identify the missing evidence or concrete example the caller needs to obtain. Keep the branch and dependent work open rather than repeatedly asking the user to guess.
