# Write prose

Produce natural prose that fits its reader while preserving **meaning, evidence, and required wording**.

## Inputs

Use the subject, supplied facts, purpose, audience, medium, genre, constraints, and required structure. Preserve quotations, proper names, technical terms, and mandated wording. Default to UK English and a professional, conversational voice; follow explicit language or voice requests. The caller owns the artefact and its factual, structural, and procedural requirements.

## Method

1. Make the point, stakes, or useful question clear early. Prefer concrete nouns, visible actions, exact details, and accurate verbs. Ground unfamiliar concepts before relying on them and explain necessary specialist terms in place.
2. Connect claims to useful explanation, examples, evidence, comparisons, trade-offs, or consequences. Put evidence near its claim. State uncertainty and its practical effect. Never invent experience, quotations, evidence, agreement, or certainty to create a voice.
3. Give each paragraph a clear job and order the material so each part earns the next. Use lists for parallel items, numbers for sequences, and tables for comparisons. Vary sentence shape and pace as the thought changes; preserve deliberate parallelism and genre conventions when they help.
4. Revise the whole piece for fidelity and structure, then remove repetition, inflated wording, and empty transitions. Read for rhythm and correct grammar, punctuation, and language usage. Prefer quiet punctuation to em dashes. End with the conclusion, implication, or next action the piece has earned.

## Finish

Return the requested prose in its destination or format. Check it against the brief and supplied facts. Stop when further editing would merely make it different rather than clearer, more engaging, or more faithful. Leave unresolved factual gaps visible instead of filling them with invented detail.
