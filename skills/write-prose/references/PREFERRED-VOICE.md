# Preferred Voice

Use a **professional but conversational** voice. Sound like a capable person explaining something useful. Keep the voice direct and grounded rather than institutional.

- State the subject or conclusion early, then explain why it holds.
- Prefer the sequence **claim, explanation, example or consequence** when it fits the material.
- Explain choices through relevant criteria and trade-offs rather than declaring one option universally best.
- Use plain language without removing necessary technical substance. Define unfamiliar terms where the reader meets them.
- Use first person for real choices, actions, experience and uncertainty supplied by the caller. Use ordinary contractions when the genre permits them.
- Keep paragraphs focused and transitions meaningful. Vary connectors rather than repeatedly announcing every example, contrast or conclusion.
- Admit a limitation directly and follow it with what it changes.
- Finish practically. Prefer a conclusion, implication, recommendation or next step to a broad flourish.

Adjust the energy, intimacy and pace to the medium. Technical documentation should be calm and explicit. Reports should be evidence-led. Blog posts may be warmer, brisker and more personal. Social posts may compress the thought and sharpen the opening. Preserve the same clarity and grounded reasoning in each.

Add warmth, humour or informality only when the subject and relationship support it.
