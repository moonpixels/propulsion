# Natural prose review

Use these patterns to find a problem in meaning or readability. They are editing signals, not proof of AI authorship. Repair the cause instead of disguising it with synonyms, arbitrary variation, or invented detail. Apply them to the audience and genre in the brief.

The paired examples are illustrative drafts. Bad examples deliberately contain the pattern under review. Each pair uses fixed illustrative facts established in its draft or explanation.

For actual revisions, treat the caller's confirmed facts as authoritative. A supplied draft can contain unsupported claims. Keeping the same subject in a revision does not require preserving those claims. Facts, measurements, sources, and experiences must come from the task's evidence. When a detail is missing, remove the unsupported claim, qualify it, or leave the gap visible.

## Claims and useful detail

### Generic opening

Start with the actual subject. Keep scene-setting only when a supplied situation helps the reader understand what follows.

```markdown
// Bad
In a world of constant change, clear communication matters. We are writing to inform you that your replacement charger will be dispatched on Thursday.

// Good
Your replacement charger will be dispatched on Thursday.
```

### Vague praise or feeling

Name the supplied behaviour. Remove praise that adds no supported behaviour or useful claim.

```markdown
// Bad
Saving a filter and reusing it on the Tasks page delivers an effortless experience.

// Good
You can save a filter and reuse it on the Tasks page.
```

### Decorative explanation

Explain a consequence within the evidence. Remove a trailing phrase that claims more than the action establishes. A participial clause can remain when it expresses a real relationship, such as water cooling and forming ice.

```markdown
// Bad
The service retries a failed request once, ensuring complete reliability.

// Good
The service retries a failed request once.
```

### Unnamed authority

Identify the available source, its finding, and the relevant criterion. Without a source, remove the attribution and assess whether the claim itself is supported. Here, appointment records are supplied, but no source supports the claim about experts. Never invent a citation to save a sentence.

```markdown
// Bad
Experts agree that our new appointments reduce waiting. Our appointment records show that the average wait fell from eight minutes to five.

// Good
Our appointment records show that the average wait fell from eight minutes to five.
```

### Unsupported strength

Use a supplied measurement instead of an intensifier. Without evidence, remove the claimed improvement. Preserve adverbs that convey a real condition, such as temporary unavailability.

```markdown
// Bad
The new appointments dramatically reduce waiting time. Our records show that the average wait fell from eight minutes to five.

// Good
Our records show that the average wait fell from eight minutes to five.
```

### Excessive hedging

Keep the uncertainty and, when known, what it means for the reader. Removing repeated hedges must not turn a forecast into a guarantee.

```markdown
// Bad
Your delivery could potentially perhaps be delayed.

// Good
Your delivery may be delayed.
```

### Empty conclusion

Finish with an earned implication or useful action. Omit a closing paragraph when the piece is already complete.

```markdown
// Bad
Delivery is expected on Monday. Reply if you would prefer a refund. We look forward to a brighter future together.

// Good
Delivery is expected on Monday. Reply if you would prefer a refund.
```

Test what each sentence helps the reader understand or do. Relevant general advice remains useful even when it applies to several products. Product claims should describe the actual product. Wider explanations should state an applicable principle or relevant observation.

## Wording and framing

### Inflated wording

Use plain words. Review words such as "leverage", "delve", "pivotal", "tapestry", and "underscore" for an unnecessary abstraction or flourish. A word's presence alone is not a defect.

```markdown
// Bad
We endeavour to facilitate your utilisation of the service.

// Good
We help you use the service.
```

### Padded identity or possession

Use the verb that names the actual relationship. An alternative to "is" can remain when it adds meaning, such as a garage serving as a workshop.

```markdown
// Bad
The venue boasts a garden and serves as a place for monthly meetings.

// Good
The venue has a garden and hosts monthly meetings.
```

### Manufactured contrast

State the supported change. Keep a contrast when readers need to distinguish real alternatives or correct a likely misunderstanding.

```markdown
// Bad
You can now choose an evening appointment. This is not just a schedule change, but a new era of flexibility.

// Good
You can now choose an evening appointment.
```

### False range

Name the actual subjects. Keep ranges with meaningful endpoints, such as ages five to eleven.

```markdown
// Bad
From trust to innovation, this guide covers it all through consent, record keeping, and complaints.

// Good
The guide explains consent, record keeping, and complaints.
```

### Decorative jargon or metaphor

Explain the action. Keep precise terms such as "vector" in a mathematical explanation and "test harness" in developer guidance. Explain a specialist term for an unfamiliar audience.

```markdown
// Bad
Putting repairs before expansion provides a powerful vector for progress.

// Good
The plan puts repairs before expansion.
```

### Mannered flourish

Use literal wording by default. Preserve an image when it does useful explanatory or expressive work for the requested audience and genre. Use the techniques reference to make that choice.

```markdown
// Bad
Friday's application deadline is a drumbeat that refuses to quieten.

// Good
Applications close on Friday.
```

### Filler

Remove introductions that add no qualification. Shorten padded phrases when nothing is lost.

```markdown
// Bad
It is important to note that you must apply by Friday in order to attend.

// Good
You must apply by Friday to attend.
```

### Automatic pleasantry or flattery

Answer the question or acknowledge the particular point. Remove routine opening enthusiasm and generic closing offers. Keep a relevant apology, thanks, or offer when the relationship and task support it.

```markdown
// Bad
Excellent question! You're completely right to ask. Applications close on Friday. I hope this helps!

// Good
Applications close on Friday.
```

## Sentences and repetition

### Synonym cycling

Repeat the precise term when several names refer to the same thing. Keep different names when they identify different concepts.

```markdown
// Bad
Save the filter. The preset is private. Rename the saved view later.

// Good
Save the filter. The filter is private. You can rename it later.
```

### Forced triad or balance

State the supported benefits, however many there are. Retain three items when there really are three, and parallel wording when it makes comparable ideas easier to follow.

```markdown
// Bad
The library will open on Saturdays, bringing clarity, confidence, and empowerment to weekend visitors.

// Good
The library will open on Saturdays, giving weekend visitors another day to use it.
```

### Dense sentence

Keep qualifications beside the claims they affect. Split material that makes the reader backtrack.

```markdown
// Bad
Although the form accepts late applications if places remain, which depends on cancellations, applicants must pay before attending and should wait for confirmation before booking travel.

// Good
Late applications are accepted if cancellations leave places available. Wait for confirmation before booking travel. You must pay before attending.
```

### Unhelpful passive

Prefer a known actor when the action matters. Passive wording is appropriate when the actor is unknown or irrelevant. Do not invent an actor.

```markdown
// Bad
Each row is checked by the loader.

// Good
The loader checks each row.
```

### Cryptic compression

Restore articles, verbs, conditions, and meaningful relationships. Preserve exact command names and established abbreviations the audience understands.

```markdown
// Bad
Any row has bad date → command exits 2, no records written.

// Good
If any row contains an invalid date, the command exits with code 2 and writes no records.
```

### Mechanical rhythm or repetition

Give each paragraph a distinct job. Merge repeated claims and vary sentence length with the explanation and emphasis. Useful repeated terminology and deliberate cadence can remain.

```markdown
// Bad
The library opens on Saturdays. This means it is open at the weekend.

Weekend visitors have another day to use the library. Saturday opening provides that extra day.

// Good
The library opens on Saturdays, giving weekend visitors another day to use it.
```

A sentence can contain a condition and its consequences while still having one clear main point. Readability does not require making every sentence equally short.

## Punctuation and presentation

Apply the punctuation and presentation policy in [the skill](../SKILL.md). Restructure a crowded sentence before choosing a replacement mark.

### Punctuation carrying too much structure

Turn an aside into a separate sentence or integrate it with commas. Rewrite an explanatory colon as "because" or split the sentence. Preserve relationships and exact source wording.

```markdown
// Bad
Your booking is confirmed (we received your payment); bring your letter: we need it to check you in.

// Good
We received your payment, so your booking is confirmed. Bring your letter so we can check you in.
```

### Em dash or substitute dash

Split or restructure the sentence. Keep a hyphen inside an established compound and a dash inside a required quotation or technical literal.

```markdown
// Bad
Your appointment is confirmed — bring your letter.

// Good
Your appointment is confirmed. Bring your letter.
```

### Repeated inline label

Remove a label that repeats the sentence. Keep a useful lead-in when it adds information. Use connected prose when the bullet format adds no scanning value.

```markdown
// Bad

- **Delivery:** Delivery is expected on Monday.

// Good
Delivery is expected on Monday.
```

### Decorative formatting

Emphasise only what helps retrieval or a decision. Keep an emoji with a useful role in the requested voice or format. Follow the destination's quote style and required formatting.

```markdown
// Bad

## ✨ Your Upcoming Appointment

Bring your **letter** to your **appointment** on **Monday**.

// Good

## Your appointment

Bring your letter to your appointment on Monday.
```

### Template-shaped structure

Give a short answer directly. Use headings for meaningful sections of longer work and lists for items readers need to scan independently.

```markdown
// Bad

## Overview

Applications close on Friday.

## Key takeaways

- Apply by Friday.

## Conclusion

Remember to apply by Friday.

// Good
Applications close on Friday.
```

Check the whole piece again after local edits. Preserve the brief, necessary reasoning, uncertainty, and required wording. A cleaner sentence must not conceal a lost fact or qualification.
