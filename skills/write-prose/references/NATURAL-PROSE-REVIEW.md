# Natural Prose Review

Inspect the piece for:

- a generic opening that delays the actual subject;
- vague claims, missing examples or detail that cannot be inspected;
- repeated words, claims, sentence openings, transitions or conclusions;
- any em dash that could become a comma, full stop, colon, semicolon or parentheses, especially when more than one appears;
- paragraphs with conspicuously uniform length or function;
- repeated balanced clauses or triads;
- inflated vocabulary, needless intensifiers or a register more complex than the reader needs;
- over-explanation, restated implications and a conclusion that merely summarises the summary;
- headings, lists and transitions applied as a template rather than demanded by the material;
- uniform formality that obscures the writer's grounded point of view;
- invented or unchecked quotations, names, dates, numbers, citations or factual claims; and
- false familiarity, personal experience or collective language not supplied by the caller.

Correct the underlying problem. Add specificity instead of decorative variation. Compress repetition instead of swapping synonyms. Change rhythm where thought and emphasis change. Preserve deliberate parallelism, repetition, technical vocabulary and genre conventions when they serve the piece.

Default to no em dashes. Keep one only when a rare, strong interruption is clearer than quieter punctuation, and rewrite if more than one appears unless each is essential. Let contractions, sentence lengths and word strength follow meaning and register.
