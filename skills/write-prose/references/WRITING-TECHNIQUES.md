# Writing Techniques

Treat these as available tools. Choose a technique because of the job it performs, not because varied prose must contain it.

## Orient the reader

- **Direct opening:** State the point or problem when the reader needs immediate utility. Avoid generic scene-setting.
- **Narrative opening:** Begin with a real supplied moment or concrete case when experience will make an abstract subject tangible. Do not invent an anecdote.
- **Reader question:** Surface a genuine objection, invite a useful prediction or create a transition the answer fulfils. Avoid stock questions and empty requests for engagement.

## Explain and persuade

- **Example:** Make a general claim inspectable. Include the boundary that prevents the example from becoming a false universal rule.
- **Comparison:** Name the criterion, show the relevant alternatives and explain the consequence of the difference.
- **Analogy:** Map an unfamiliar idea to a familiar one, state where the mapping stops and retire it once direct explanation can carry the point.
- **Quotation:** Use exact words only when their wording or speaker matters. Attribute, contextualise and interpret them; paraphrase when only the information matters.
- **Direct address:** Use _you_ for a real reader action or relationship and _we_ only for genuine shared ground. Avoid synthetic intimacy or presumed agreement.

## Control pace and emphasis

- **Sentence contrast:** Give explanation room, then use a short sentence to land a real conclusion. Do not manufacture a short-long-short pattern.
- **Parallel phrasing or a triad:** Clarify genuinely comparable ideas or create a brief cadence. Do not pad a list to three or repeat the same pattern nearby.
- **Fragment, one-sentence paragraph or repetition:** Reserve it for an emphasis the surrounding prose has earned.
- **Humour or an aside:** Add local warmth or relief when it fits the writer's role and subject. Keep it subordinate to the message.

## Expose structure

- **Heading:** Name a useful stage or question in a longer piece. Avoid uniform template headings that could belong to any subject.
- **Bullets:** Use for related items readers may scan independently.
- **Numbers:** Use for sequence, rank or a fixed count.
- **Prose:** Keep connected reasoning in sentences and paragraphs when relationships and qualification matter more than item retrieval.

Use punctuation to show relationships and pace. A full stop can isolate a conclusion, a colon can introduce an explanation and a semicolon can hold close independent clauses together. Use an em dash sparingly for a distinct interruption or aside; when several appear, check whether full stops, commas, colons or parentheses express the relationships better.
